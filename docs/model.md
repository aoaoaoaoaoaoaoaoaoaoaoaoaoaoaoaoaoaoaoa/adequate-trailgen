# Model

The core noun is the `TrailGraph`: vertices, bidirectional edges, edge geometry, and attributed edge costs. Source geometries are split at planar segment intersections. Near-miss snapping is deliberately narrow: a dangling endpoint may be projected onto another segment only when the projection falls inside that segment and within `snap_tolerance_m`. Edges adjacent to such synthetic junctions carry `graph-builder` / `near-miss-snap` provenance and lower confidence. Multiedges are permitted; direction is deliberately absent until a source adapter can prove one-way travel.

`trailgen build` can normalize GeoJSON or shapefile trail/network layers, or a single supplied route file. GPX, KML, KMZ, CSV, and route GeoJSON build inputs become low-confidence `SegmentDraft` scaffolds with `route-file` / `route-derived-network` provenance. That path is for bootstrapping and personal-route analysis; source coverage still records the input as a seed route, not as a satisfied authoritative trail-network layer.

Each edge stores length, ascent, descent, mean/max grade, sustained steep distance, fixed-bin grade distribution, terrain bucket, raw source surface tag when present, terrain evidence, access status, road and water crossing evidence, road exposure, confidence, scalar difficulty, persisted difficulty breakdown, elevation provenance, and source provenance. The grade distribution bins edge meters into flat `<5%`, rolling `5–15%`, steep `15–30%`, and savage `≥30%` absolute grade. The scalar difficulty model is additive and configurable: distance, elevation gain/loss, grade, per-bucket terrain multipliers, road exposure, confidence penalty, and access penalty.

Elevation enrichment is a separate phase. `ElevationSampler` is the trait seam for embedded geometry elevations, synthetic fixtures, local Arc/Info ASCII Grid DEMs, north-up geographic single-band GeoTIFF DEMs, and future VRT/projected-raster samplers. Enrichment densifies geometries at configurable spacing, samples elevation, recomputes ascent/descent and grade statistics, then records which source produced the elevation values.

Terrain inference is evidence-bearing: explicit source tags win with high confidence; unknown surfaces may be inferred from road exposure or sampled grade, but those inferences carry lower confidence and a rationale string. Terrain overlays are an intermediate-confidence evidence source for land-cover layers, surface maps, and user overrides. Polygon overlays affect edges by midpoint containment; line overlays affect nearby edges within `tolerance_m`. Applied overlays update the edge terrain bucket, append terrain evidence, lower confidence according to overlay confidence, and recompute difficulty.

Imported seed routes are first-class project artifacts. A seed stores source path, format, point count, snapped edge IDs, loop-closure status, metrics, and provenance. Seed imports raise `seed_count`, `popularity`, and confidence on touched edges, and closed connected seeds are included as route candidates during generation.

Access overlays are applied after graph construction. A GeoJSON overlay feature declares `access` or `status`, confidence, provenance fields, and either a Polygon/MultiPolygon area or a LineString corridor. Polygon overlays affect edges whose midpoint lies inside the area; line overlays affect nearby edges within `tolerance_m`. Applied overlays set edge access status, record access provenance, lower confidence, and recompute difficulty.

Context overlays are also applied after graph construction. GeoJSON or shapefile road/hydrology LineString features declare `kind` or `context`; shapefile filenames such as `roads.shp` or `streams.shp` can supply the context kind when DBF rows omit it. Segment intersections with graph edges are counted as crossings. Road crossings raise road-exposure hints, road and water crossings carry provenance, and route metrics aggregate crossing counts for reports and exports.

Route metrics classify shape as `Loop`, `FigureEight`, `OutAndBack`, or `Open`. Shape is measured from the traversed vertex/edge sequence, not from a caller-supplied label: repeated edges imply out-and-back, repeated junctions inside a closed route imply figure-eight, and non-closing routes are open. Each route measures terrain mix, access mix, road/pavement exposure, low-confidence exposure, restricted-access exposure, and distance-weighted repeated-edge exposure. `road_fraction` is the union of explicit road-exposure hints and terrain-normalized `road`/`pavement` edges. Each `Route` carries a persisted scalar score plus an integer Pareto rank. Lower scores are better inside a front, while lower Pareto ranks mean the route is less dominated across constraint penalty, window deviations, scalar difficulty, road/pavement exposure, low-confidence exposure, restricted-access exposure, and repeated-edge exposure.

The loop hunter is currently a bounded edge-simple DFS with shape-filtered closed candidates, deliberate figure-eight continuation through the start when that shape is allowed, and a mirrored out-and-back branch when that shape is allowed. It is behind the `RouteSolver` trait (`LoopHunter` + `SearchParams`) so a later annealer, k-shortest hybrid, MILP, or CP-SAT backend can replace it without rewriting graph or report code.
