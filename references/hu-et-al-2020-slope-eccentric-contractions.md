## Synopsis

The authors recreate musculoskeletal simulations of healthy participants
walking at 1.25 m/s on level ground and ±3°, ±6°, and ±9° slopes. They integrate
negative active-fiber power across 35 lower-limb muscles as a measure of
eccentric contraction.

Total normalized negative muscle work increased by 13% at −3°, 19% at −6°,
and 32% at −9° relative to level walking. Low uphill grades of +3° and +6° were
similar to level; +9° increased total negative muscle work by 17%. Knee
extensors account for much of the downhill increase. Joint negative work and
hip coactivation together explain much of the variation in modeled muscle
negative work.

## Source Assessment

The work supports an asymmetric grade model and confirms that downhill walking
cannot be treated as metabolically cheap level travel. It studies simulated
eccentric work, not delayed soreness or injury, and was motivated by exercise
management in Duchenne muscular dystrophy. Its healthy-participant source data
and fixed speed still make it a bounded mechanics reference rather than a
general hiking-damage law.
