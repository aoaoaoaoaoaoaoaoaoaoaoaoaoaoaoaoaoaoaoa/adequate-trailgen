## Synopsis

Eighteen recreational team-sport athletes completed running and jumping
protocols designed to produce low- or high-impact lower-extremity mechanics.
Ground-reaction forces confirmed that absorption mechanics differed.
Participants separately rated breathlessness (`RPE-B`) and leg-muscle exertion
(`RPE-L`) on the CR100 scale after each bout.

Leg-muscle exertion was 6.50 arbitrary units higher in the low-impact session,
but breathlessness was also 4.96 units higher there. The authors conclude that
high versus low lower-extremity impact does not explain the separation between
the two differential ratings.

## Source Assessment

The study supports differential RPE as a way to ask about different perceived
components, but rejects interpreting leg RPE as a direct impact or
musculoskeletal-force sensor. It is a small laboratory study of running and
jumping, not hiking.
