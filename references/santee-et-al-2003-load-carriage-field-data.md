## Synopsis

Eight male subjects walked graded gravel roads at 1.34 m/s with loads of 0, 13.6, or 27.2 kg. The tested algorithms agreed with most field subsets when using the conventional dirt-road factor 1.1. At +8.6% grade under load, observed cost exceeded prediction; 1.3 fit that subset, but overpredicted others. The authors reject inferring a universal revised coefficient from the small sample and identify roughness, traction, speed, load, and grade as interacting causes.

## Source Assessment

The study supplies useful field validation and an explicit warning against rigid terrain categories. Its pace, loads, military population, and small sample differ from ordinary recreational hiking.
