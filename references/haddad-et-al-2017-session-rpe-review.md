## Synopsis

This review examines studies published from 2001 through 2016 that use Foster's
session rating of perceived exertion method. Thirty-six studies assess its
validity or reliability across several sports, ages, sexes, and expertise
levels. The reviewed evidence generally supports the method as a practical
internal training-load measure.

An athlete rates the mean intensity of the completed session on a modified Borg
CR10 scale. Session load is the rating multiplied by session duration in
minutes and is reported in arbitrary units. The method deliberately integrates
duration and perceived intensity rather than measuring physical work.

The review identifies material influences on perceived exertion, including
fitness, experience, psychological state, environment, temperature, altitude,
feedback, and substances. It recommends individualization and, where useful,
complementary physiological or external measurements.

## Source Assessment

Session-RPE is an established retrospective measure of perceived training
load. It is not a prospective route model, a tissue-specific force measure, or
an injury-risk estimator. Multiplication by duration is a monitoring
convention, not a physical unit.
