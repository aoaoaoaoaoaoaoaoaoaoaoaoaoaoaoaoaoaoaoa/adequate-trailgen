## Synopsis

Eleven healthy young adults walked at 1.0 m/s over smooth and manufactured
uneven surfaces with height variation up to 2.5 cm. Uneven ground shortened
steps by 4%, increased step-length and step-width variability by 22% and 36%,
and increased activation in several thigh and lower-leg muscles.

Relative to smooth ground, uneven terrain increased positive knee work by 28%,
positive hip work by 62%, and the magnitude of negative knee work by 26%.
Metabolic power increased by 28%. The authors attribute much of the energetic
change to greater lower-limb mechanical work, with muscle coactivation and
stabilization as additional plausible contributors.

## Source Assessment

The experiment demonstrates that modest unevenness materially alters joint
work and muscle activation. It supplies one defensible terrain-effect anchor,
not a taxonomy of natural surfaces: the manufactured pattern, fixed speed,
small sample, and limited acclimation do not justify extrapolating exact
multipliers to talus, mud, scrambling, or bushwhacking.
