## Synopsis

Eight participants, six male and two female, walked at 1.25 m/s on a treadmill
at −15%, −10%, 0%, +10%, and +15% grades and ran at 2.25 m/s over a narrower
grade range. Motion capture and force data supplied average positive and
negative mechanical power at the ankle, knee, and hip.

For walking, total positive and negative lower-limb joint power in W/kg were:

| Grade | Positive | Negative |
|------:|---------:|---------:|
| −15% | 0.86 | −2.60 |
| −10% | 0.94 | −1.60 |
| 0% | 1.02 | −1.03 |
| +10% | 1.71 | −0.71 |
| +15% | 2.60 | −0.62 |

Positive power shifts toward the hip uphill. Negative power increases sharply
downhill and is dominated by the knee. The study's purpose is to guide
lower-limb exoskeleton assistance across grades, not to predict fatigue or
injury.

## Source Assessment

The table supplies a rare mass-normalized, direction-sensitive external
mechanics curve suitable for a population route-dose prior. Summed joint work
is not muscle work, tissue damage, discomfort, or capacity; biarticular energy
transfer and co-contraction limit that interpretation. The sample and fixed
treadmill speed are small and narrow.
