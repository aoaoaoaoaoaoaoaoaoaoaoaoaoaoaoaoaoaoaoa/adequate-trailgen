## Synopsis

The authors compare established hiking-speed functions with 7,636 walking tracks from England and Wales: more than 1.4 million observations spanning almost 88,000 km. Their generalized linear model includes grade in the direction of travel, transverse hillside grade, paved or unpaved road status, and off-road obstruction. Each factor materially affects local speed.

The new model reduces local prediction error, especially on traverses and obstructed terrain. Whole-route errors differ less: all compared models average 13.5–15.5% error because local over- and underestimates cancel. Naismith's rule remains useful for total route time but overstates ascent time and understates descent time. Tobler's usual 0.6 off-road speed multiplier is too severe for this data.

Crowdsourced tracks, imperfect surface classification, sparse steep-terrain observations, and selection of terrain that users could in fact walk bound the result.

## Source Assessment

The work supplies a strong population-scale travel-time model, not an energy or perceived-exertion model. Its UK surface classes are coarser than Trailgen's terrain ontology.
