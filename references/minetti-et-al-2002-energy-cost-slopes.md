## Synopsis

Ten trained mountain runners walked and ran on a treadmill at grades from −45% through +45%. The paper reports metabolic energy per kilogram and meter at the energy-minimizing walking speed for each tested grade. Walking cost is U-shaped downhill: 1.64 J·kg⁻¹·m⁻¹ on level ground, 0.81 at −10%, then 3.46 at −45%. Uphill cost rises to 4.68 at +10%, 8.07 at +20%, and 17.33 at +45%. Table 2 supplies measured means and standard deviations; Figure 1 supplies fifth-order regressions.

The experiment isolates slope on a treadmill. It does not measure rough ground, technical risk, navigation, delayed soreness from eccentric loading, weather, or an ordinary hiking population. The downhill running results are unusually economical relative to sedentary subjects.

## Source Assessment

This is the most direct retained source for a grade-dependent energy-equivalent distance. The small, trained, male sample and treadmill substrate preclude treating its coefficients as universal hiking difficulty.
