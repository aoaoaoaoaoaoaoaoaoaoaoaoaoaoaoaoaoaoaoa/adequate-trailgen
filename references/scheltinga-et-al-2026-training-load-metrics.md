## Synopsis

Twelve experienced runners completed an endurance run, a submaximal five
kilometer run, and interval training outdoors. The authors compared subjective
session rating of perceived exertion (`sRPE`), heart-rate-derived training
impulse (`TRIMP`), and weighted cumulative biomechanical load estimated from
inertial-sensor-derived peak vertical ground-reaction forces.

The biomechanical measure sums the ninth powers of detected force peaks. The
exponent follows a mechanical-fatigue model and prior Achilles-tendon work, but
the authors warn that one exponent across people and tissues may oversimplify
damage and magnifies sensor error.

`sRPE` distinguished the endurance session from the two harder sessions;
`TRIMP` and weighted cumulative load did not. Within-subject correlations were
0.49 between `sRPE` and `TRIMP`, 0.25 between `sRPE` and biomechanical load, and
0.35 between biomechanical load and `TRIMP`; only the first was statistically
significant. The measures therefore capture complementary or inadequate
projections rather than interchangeable values.

## Source Assessment

The small running study does not validate a hiking or injury-prediction score.
It directly tests the attractive assumption that subjective, physiological,
and force-based load measures agree, and finds that they do not. Its
biomechanical measure also requires per-step sensors and cannot be recovered
from route geometry alone.
