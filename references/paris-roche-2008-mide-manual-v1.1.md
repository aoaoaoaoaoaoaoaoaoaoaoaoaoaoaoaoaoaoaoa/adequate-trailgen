## Synopsis

MIDE is a standardized communication method for hiking-route demands. It
reports route identity, distance, ascent, descent, season or conditions, and
technical difficulties, then grades four independent aspects from one through
five: environmental severity, navigational difficulty, movement difficulty,
and required effort.

Movement difficulty describes the substrate and required locomotion:

1. smooth surfaces, vehicle tracks, regular stairs, sand, or gravel;
2. regular bridle paths or uniform off-trail ground;
3. irregular steps, rough off-trail ground, boulder fields, or unstable scree;
4. hands required for balance;
5. climbing moves required for progress.

For heterogeneous routes, movement and navigation are assigned from the
highest-grade material section rather than accumulated over all distance.
Effort is derived from effective walking time: one is at most one hour, two is
one to three hours, three is three to six, four is six to ten, and five is more
than ten. The timing procedure combines horizontal travel with ascent and
descent allowances.

## Source Assessment

MIDE cleanly separates terrain, navigation, hazards, and duration. Its movement
axis is a useful route-character descriptor, not an extensive
musculoskeletal-load score: one kilometer and fifty kilometers of grade-three
ground receive the same movement grade. Its effort axis is a time class.
