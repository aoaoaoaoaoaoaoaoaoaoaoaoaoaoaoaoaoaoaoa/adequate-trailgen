## Synopsis

Higgins fits all three parameters of a generalized Tobler hiking function by
nonlinear least squares to 14,449 one-second observations from four personal
hikes in Hong Kong. The traces span dirt paths, paved paths, pavement, and
stairs; pauses and an urban-canyon approach are removed. The fitted maximum
speed, slope sensitivity, and grade offset are stable across several temporal
aggregation schemes but less stable after sparse grade binning.

The fitted curve peaks around 4.6 km/h at −3.3% grade and predicts about
4.4 km/h on level ground. Relative to canonical Tobler, it is slower on flatter
ground and faster on steeper grades. The author attributes the latter partly to
stairs, illustrating that an individual's observed slope response can absorb
route infrastructure as well as physiology.

## Source Assessment

This is a transparent single-person demonstration rather than population
validation. It establishes that personal traces can eventually identify more
than a global speed factor, while also warning that terrain mix confounds a
purportedly personal slope curve.
