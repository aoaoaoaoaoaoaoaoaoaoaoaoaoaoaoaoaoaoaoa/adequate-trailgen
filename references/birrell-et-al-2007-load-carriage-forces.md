## Synopsis

Fifteen male participants completed eight military load-carriage conditions
using boots, a rifle, webbing, backpacks, and combined systems up to 40 kg.
Loads added in 8 kg increments produced approximately proportional increases
in vertical and anteroposterior ground-reaction-force measures. Carrying a
rifle separately altered impact and mediolateral impulse, illustrating that
load distribution matters in addition to mass.

## Source Assessment

The result supports total carried mass as a first-order external-load factor.
It does not establish a hiking-fatigue score, a safe load threshold, or
linearity of tissue damage. The all-male military sample and heavy equipment
conditions are unlike ordinary day hiking.
